### Dataset Summary and Purpose

LawFlow: Collecting and Simulating Lawyers' Thought Processes 

The purpose of this dataset is aim for improving the legal reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models, specifically on the entity formation tasks.

### Languages

Legal data in NLP field in English

## Dataset Structure

### Data Instances

```
{
'scenario_id':1,
'scenario':"XYZ Scenario Description",
'plan': "1. High-level Plan Step One 2. High Level Plan Step Two.....",
'agent':"human" / "llm",  
'agent_id': 0,
       'execution': [{
                      'step_id': 1,
                      'current_task': "Sub-task Name",
                      'action_taken': "[ONLY FOR HUMANS] Note_Change (Note taking Action Taken by Human)",
                      'task_content': "Alpha wants to open an LLC for...."
                      'next_task':"Name of next Sub-task executed",
                      'task_type':"[ONLY FOR HUMANS] Interactive",
                      'llm_reasoning':"[ONLY FOR LLMS] <LLM_Thinking_Block> ... </LLM_Thinking_Block>",
                      'llm_complete_output': "[ONLY FOR LLMS] <Output> ... </Output>
                     }]}
}
```
### Data Fields

- `scenario_id`: Entity formation scenario ID  (int64)
- `scenario`: Entity formation scenario description (string)
- `plan`: High level human generated plan (object, string)
- `agent`: the source of the reasoning data (Either Human or LLM). (object, string)
- `agent_id`: The ID of the human author or the LLM model used to generate the data. (Object, int64)
- `execution`: Array of low-level data collected during the execution of a particular step from the plan by an agent. (Object, list)
- `step_id`: Index of the current step sorted in order of execution  (int64)
- `current_task`: Name of the sub-task executed from the high-level plan(Object, string)
- `action_taken`: Low-level action-taken by the human agents in the current step. Null for LLMs. (Object, string)
- `task_content`: Content details of the action taken by the human or the simulated action details for the LLM. (Object, string)
- `next_task`: The task executed after the current task. (Object, string)
- `task_type`: The type of the task such as: interactive/observable/instrospective/ (Object, string)
- `llm_reasoning`: The reasoning trace of the simulated actions generated by the LLM.Null for Humans.(Object, string)
- `llm_complete_output`: The complete output generated by the LLM comprising of 'content', 'llm_reasoning', and the next suggested step to execute.Null for Humans.(Object, string)

## Dataset Creation

### Curation Rationale

We present <span style="font-variant: small-caps;">LawFlow-Reasoning-Simulation</span> , a curated dataset of 2,009 reasoning actions in the legal domain, annotated by experts in law and further simulated using LLMs on realistic scenarios. We develop a taxonomy of scholarly writing intentions, providing an overall understanding of how lawyers tend to tackle complex Entity formation tasks.
### Source Data

#### Initial Data Collection and Normalization

##### Human:

We designed and implemented a web application, which enables the real-time collection of user-data through keystrokes and other high-level actions taken within the app. Participants are given their account credentials, and after logging into the system, the application logs the user's actions in the background silently, without disrupting the typical legal reasoning process.

The application logs user actions when one of these actions occurs: 

1. Inserting or deleting characters
2. Copy/paste  
3. Google Search Queries and results (through API in the application)
4. Downloading files from the in-app document library
5. Vector search queries and results within the in-app document library

The application sends the user-log array along with other metadata (e.g., timestamp, author ID, action name, etc.) to the backend server.

##### LLM:

For the LLM generated data, we use the same 10 entity formation scenarios implemented by the human authors. The LLM is given the follows as context:

1. Scenario Description
2. High-Level Human Generated Plan

Following this, the LLM is asked to reason about the context given to it and asked to choose the step it would execute from the given plan. The LLM then also generates the execution details by either simulating a conversation between the lawyer and the client or generating only lawyer specific actions. The LLM then suggests the next step to execute. This suggested step always executed next. From here on, the prompt changes to only generating the execution details of the chosen step and generates the next step to execute as well. The output of the earlier steps are given as context to the prompt as well.

#### Who are the source language producers?

##### Human:
We recruited 4 Law-school graduate students, all of them currently attend a university in the U.S. and are proficient in
English. We collect data from September 2024 to January 2025, totaling up to 5 months. 

##### LLM:
We used reasoning models such as OpenAI O1 and DeepSeek R1.

## Considerations for Using the Data

### Limitations
First, the  <span style="font-variant: small-caps;">LawFlow-Reasoning-Simulation</span>  dataset is currently limited to the Entity Formation legal task. This task-specific focus may restrict the dataset's generalizability to other legal tasks. Future work could address this limitation by collecting data from a broader range of legal tasks.

Second, our dataset includes contributions from only 4 participants on 10 scenarios and simulation data from 2 LLMs on 10 scenarios as well. This small sample size is partly due to the complex and expensive process of analysing legal tasks. Future studies could explore more robust data collection protocols and this dataset does encourage gathering simulation data on more scenarios from various LLMs.

Furthermore, all participants were early-career researchers (e.g., Graduate Law students) at an R1 university in the United States. Expanding the dataset to include veterans, such as practicing lawyers and law professors, could offer valuable insights into how lawyers think.

Despite these limitations, our study captured an end-to-end execution of a complex legal task 4 unique authors and 2 LLMs, resulting in a diverse range of reasoning patterns. The dataset contains approximately 2,004 samples, offering fine-grained insights into the human and LLM reasoning process, including detailed editing and drafting actions over time. While the number of scenarios is limited, the granularity and volume of the data provide a rich resource for understanding thinking behaviors. 

Third, collaborative legal execution of tasks is currently not well defined and captured in our dataset, as only one lawyer focused on logging in the data in the application despite having multiple lawyers during the client interaction processes. This limits our ability to analyze collaborative dynamics, which are common in law. Future work should prioritize collecting multi-author projects to better capture these dynamics. Additionally, the dataset is exclusive to English-language, which restricts its applicability to multilingual or non-English legal contexts. Expanding to multilingual settings could reveal unique cognitive insights into legal reasoning across languages.
