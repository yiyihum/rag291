# (NeurIPS 2024 Oral) Aligner: Efficient Alignment by Learning to Correct

This repository hosts *AlignerTails*, an extended dataset from our NeurIPS 2024 paper [Aligner: Efficient Alignment by Learning to Correct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02416). Incorporating prompts, answers and corrections generated by state-of-the-art models like GPT-4o and refined by human annotators, it serves as a robust resource for exploring the correction paradigm and advancing alignment research.

Work done by [PKU-Alignment Team](https://github.com/PKU-Alignment)


## Abstract
With the rapid development of large language models (LLMs) and ever-evolving practical requirements, finding an efficient and effective alignment method has never been more critical. However, the tension between the complexity of current alignment methods and the need for rapid iteration in deployment scenarios necessitates the development of a model-agnostic alignment approach that can operate under these constraints. In this paper, we introduce *Aligner*, a novel and simple alignment paradigm that learns the correctional residuals between preferred and dispreferred answers using a small model.
Designed as a model-agnostic, plug-and-play module, *Aligner* can be directly applied to various open-source and API-based models with only one-off training, making it suitable for rapid iteration.
Notably, *Aligner* can be applied to any powerful, large-scale upstream models. 
Moreover, it can even iteratively bootstrap the upstream models using corrected responses as synthetic human preference data, breaking through the model's performance ceiling.
Our experiments demonstrate performance improvements by deploying the same *Aligner* model across 11 different LLMs, evaluated on the 3H dimensions (helpfulness, harmlessness, and honesty).
Specifically, *Aligner*-7B has achieved an average improvement of 68.9\% in helpfulness and 22.8\% in harmlessness across the tested LLMs while also effectively reducing hallucination.
In the Alpaca-Eval leaderboard, stacking *Aligner*-2B on GPT-4 Turbo improved its LC Win Rate from 55.0\% to 58.3\%, surpassing GPT-4 Omni's 57.5\% Win Rate (community report).

## Dataset Summary

This dataset is a sibling project of [Aligner](https://pku-aligner.github.io/). 

We present a high-quality dataset comprising over 100K Q-A-C (Question-Answer-Correction) pairs across three critical dimensions: **harmlessness**, **helpfulness**, and **honesty**. The dataset encompasses tasks spanning a wide range of topics, including mathematics, empathy, safety, summarization, planning, and more. This dataset is suitable for exploring the **correction paradigm** or as a **general preference dataset**.

The prompts are collected from publicly available datasets such as [BeaverTails](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PKU-Alignment/BeaverTails), [PKU-SafeRLHF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PKU-Alignment/PKU-SafeRLHF), [HH-RLHF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Anthropic/hh-rlhf), [UltraFeedback](https://huggingface.co/datasets/openbmb/UltraFeedback), among others.

The original answers are generated using various models, including Llama2-7B, Llama3-8B, and GPT-4 Turbo, covering both API-based and open-source models. Subsequently, we leverage GPT-4 Turbo and Llama2-70B-Instruct to generate corrected answers. Additionally, human annotators review and correct the answers to ensure the dataset's high quality.

With the rapid advancements in large language models, newer models such as Llama3-8B, GPT-4o, and GPT-4o-mini have emerged. To further **foster exploration of the correction paradigm** within the community, we have expanded the dataset by incorporating a broader range of prompts covering topics like mathematics, summarization, and planning. The original answers were generated using these newer models, and corrected answers were produced with the aid of powerful models like GPT-4o. Human annotators further refined these corrections to maintain the dataset's reliability and accuracy.

This process has resulted in the creation of the *AlignerTails* dataset. While this dataset extends beyond the scope of our NeurIPS paper, we believe it will serve as a valuable resource for the community and further enhance research in this domain.

We will continue to update this dataset and open-source more advanced and high-quality aligners to support the community's progress.


## Usage

To load our dataset, use the `load_dataset()` function as follows:

```python
from datasets import load_dataset

dataset = load_dataset("aligner/alignertails")
```

To load a specified subset of our dataset, add the `data_dir` parameter. For example:

```python
from datasets import load_dataset

dataset = load_dataset("aligner/alignertails", name='safety')
```
